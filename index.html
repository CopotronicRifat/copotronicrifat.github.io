Introduction

About Me

I am a fifth-year Ph.D. Candidate in Computer Science at Oklahoma State University , working with Dr. Atriya Sen in the Reasoning and Artificial Intelligence (rAIson) Lab . I advanced to doctoral candidacy in September 2026 after successfully completing my Ph.D. qualifying examination. I expect to graduate in July 2027.

Research. My dissertation, Adaptive Computation in Transformers: From Selective Information Processing to Causal Mechanistic Interpretability , investigates how language models allocate computation, retain task-relevant information, and support reliable reasoning. My broader research spans parameter-efficient adaptation, multimodal representation learning, symbolic planning, and causal and counterfactual analysis.

My recent work includes dynamic low-rank routing, adaptive token and memory selection, emergent controller modules in transformers, and causal analysis of sentiment shifts. My next research direction focuses on sparse autoencoders and causal interventions to examine the mechanisms behind adaptive computation. My work has appeared in ICLR, EMNLP, and Findings of ACL and EACL.

Teaching and mentoring. I support undergraduate instruction at Oklahoma State University and previously served as a lecturer and instructor of record at University of Asia Pacific and Uttara University . My teaching experience includes machine learning, algorithms, operating systems, programming, and discrete mathematics. I have also supervised undergraduate projects and mentored students through experimental research and scientific writing. I earned my B.Sc. in Computer Science and Engineering from Rajshahi University of Engineering and Technology (RUET) .

Academic goals. I seek to establish an independent research program in efficient, interpretable, and trustworthy artificial intelligence, combining rigorous experimentation with inclusive teaching, student mentorship, and reproducible scholarship.

Faculty job market: I am seeking tenure-track Assistant Professor positions in Computer Science beginning in Fall 2027.

    Compared SVIR-based numerical solvers, including the midpoint and fourth-order Runge–Kutta methods, with LSTM, CNN, FTA-LSTM, and Random Forest models for forecasting H1N1 outbreaks. Random Forest with direct forecasting produced the strongest performance on the evaluated real-world data.

    Code
  • Sentiment Analysis on Cloud Platforms (2022)

    Code

    Developed and evaluated Inception, VGG16, MobileNet, transformer, and other deep-learning architectures for detecting invasive ductal carcinoma in histopathology images from the Kaggle IDC dataset.

    Code

    Developed an Xception-based pipeline for extracting, visualizing, and classifying image embeddings, including interactive two- and three-dimensional TensorBoard visualizations.
